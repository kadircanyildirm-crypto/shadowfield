Every field below was found without a decoder: candidate byte readings were tested against sunlight, thermal lag, orbital phase, wall-clock time and the IGRF magnetic field computed from the TLE. Names, units and scales are Claude's reading of that evidence, with the satellite name withheld.

Frame type raw:len72:b0=0x09

360 frames, 81 passes, 72-byte payload

A 72-byte beacon frame with a small header, several slowly varying single-byte fields, and a number of multi-byte fields that look like float or packed data misread as integers. Evidence is weak: most fields are flat or quantised, and several correlations come from the lack of eclipse variety (every frame follows an eclipse out of view).

Health. Not enough evidence. No counters or clocks were identified, so reboots cannot be assessed, and no field is confidently a battery or temperature. Drift values are tiny or not meaningful over 13.5 days.

Frame type raw:len72:b0=0x84

331 frames, 74 passes, 72-byte payload

A 72-byte housekeeping beacon with a Unix timestamp, a solar-input-like field, several signed temperature-like bytes that follow sunlight, a rolling sequence counter, and a few small bytes of uncertain meaning. Every frame had an eclipse in the preceding 80 minutes, so the sun/shadow contrasts are real physics.

Health. No timestamp resets are seen, and the counter wraps are expected for a one-byte sequence number. Panel temperatures swing about 15 degC between sun and shadow, which is normal. There is not enough evidence for battery health, because no clear battery voltage was identified.
