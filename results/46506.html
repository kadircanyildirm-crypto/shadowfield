NetSat (blind test): telemetry recovered from orbital physics

Every field below was found without a decoder: candidate byte readings were tested against sunlight, thermal lag, orbital phase, wall-clock time and the IGRF magnetic field computed from the TLE. Names, units and scales are Claude's reading of that evidence, with the satellite name withheld.

1724 frames, 241 passes, 89-byte payload

15 of 22 weeks had no eclipse in the hours before any frame; they are left out of the sunlight correlations (but still used for counters, clocks, GNSS and orbit phase), and correlations are taken within weeks, so seasonal drift cannot pose as physics.

Every frame was heard in sunlight, but many came minutes after an eclipse (the shadow falls where there are no ground stations): sunlight history still separates the fields.

An 89-byte beacon frame with several multi-byte counters, odd byte-aligned fields that look like parts of larger values, and a few fields correlated with time since an out-of-view eclipse. Many candidate fields are probably misaligned slices of the same counters and flags, so the layout is uncertain.

Health. The counters at offsets 22 and 45 reset 10 times in 150 days, the last about 59.5 days before the end, which suggests repeated reboots or counter wraps. The reset count is uncertain because the counters are rolling and may wrap by design. There is not enough evidence on battery or thermal health.
